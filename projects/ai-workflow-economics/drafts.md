# AI workflow economics publication drafts

## Blog draft | The real cost of an AI-generated first draft

How much does AI save on a task? Start by measuring what it costs to produce work someone can actually use.

Drafting is one part of the job. Someone still has to verify the figures, check that the output answers the question, and fix errors. These steps can absorb the time AI saved at the start.

Imagine a task that takes 30 minutes manually at a labor cost of $60 an hour. An AI-assisted draft takes 10 minutes and costs $2 in tool fees. In this illustrative example, the assisted workflow breaks even at 18 minutes of review and rework, assuming the two workflows deliver work of equal quality.

That threshold changes how a team would judge the tool. A fast first draft is valuable when the team can get it through review efficiently. For a task that needs specialized verification, review may be where most of the cost sits.

Pick one recurring task and time it from assignment to acceptance. Where would the time saved go, and what would it take to keep the quality you need?

## Policy memo draft | Measure accepted outputs in AI procurement pilots

**Audience** — Public-sector procurement and program leads.

**Recommendation** — Require any workflow pilot used to justify a wider rollout to report cost per accepted output, first-pass acceptance, and material errors against the current process.

A speed-only pilot can miss costs borne by reviewers or by the people who rely on the finished work. Existing manual-process records offer a cheaper comparison when they have consistent timing and quality measures. A paired trial is preferable when task difficulty varies or baseline records are weak. Either approach takes staff time and may delay the rollout decision.

Start with one repeatable workflow. Specify the acceptance rubric and material-error criteria in advance, document who verifies outputs, and count rework and failed attempts in the cost measure. Reconsider expansion if the workflow fails the quality criteria or its cost advantage disappears after including review and rework.
