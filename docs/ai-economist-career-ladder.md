# AI Economist job descriptions and career ladder

U.S. market reference, reviewed November 18, 2026. This is a proposed job family informed by actual employer examples. Titles, degrees, and decision authority vary across organizations. The ladder describes increasing responsibility and gives hiring signals for each level. Follow the employer's requirements when applying.

## What an AI economist does

**Role purpose.** Measure how AI changes productivity, jobs, wages, prices, competition, financial decisions, and economic welfare. Translate those measurements into decisions about adoption, investment, business strategy, and policy. Some positions study AI's economic impacts, some evaluate AI inside a firm, and some use AI as a research tool. Read the posting to identify the primary mandate.

| Responsibility | Typical output |
| --- | --- |
| Measure adoption and exposure | Task and occupation indicators, adoption dashboard, and documentation of coverage and measurement error |
| Estimate effects and explain mechanisms | Experiment or quasi-experimental study of labor, cost, quality, demand, or distributional outcomes |
| Evaluate firm and market economics | Cost per accepted output, AI investment scenarios, pricing analysis, or competition assessment |
| Forecast and communicate uncertainty | Productivity, investment, wage, or employment scenarios with assumptions, ranges, and an evaluation date |
| Advise and publish | Decision memo, policy brief, working paper, presentation, article, or public commentary tied to evidence |
| Validate AI-assisted research | Benchmark against human or conventional methods, reproduce calculations, document model and data versions, and review outputs |

Promotion hinges on the scope of decisions you can own and evidence you can defend. Early-career work can start with clean data and credible replication. Research economist work calls for independent study design. Senior and executive work adds an agenda, team development, and institutional accountability.

## Compare the work with other economist specialties

| Specialty | Conventional work and skills | AI economist application and added requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labor economist | Estimate effects on employment, wages, mobility, skills, and inequality using worker and employer data. | Measure AI exposure, adoption, substitution, and augmentation separately. Pair task taxonomies and usage measures with credible labor-outcome data. |
| Microeconomist / Industrial organization economist | Estimate demand, analyze prices and incentives, and assess competition and welfare. | Model AI product pricing, switching costs, market structure, and workflow adoption. Measure tool costs and output quality alongside speed. |
| Financial economist | Study asset prices, credit, risk, and investment decisions using financial econometrics and valuation. | Evaluate AI investment returns, validate AI-assisted credit or financial forecasts, and assess model and market risks. Use point-in-time data and benchmark for leakage and stability. |
| Macroeconomist | Forecast growth, inflation, or employment and analyze policy using time series and structural models. | Translate task and firm evidence into economy-wide scenarios. Model diffusion, complementary investment, and heterogeneous effects. |
| Public / Development economist | Evaluate policy, incidence, public spending, and institutional constraints. | Measure who can access and benefit from AI, test public procurement choices, and evaluate policy across regions and income groups. |
| Applied econometrician / Research economist | Design studies, build models, and publish reproducible evidence. | Validate LLM-assisted coding, extraction, and classification against benchmark data. Track model versions, failure modes, and human review. |

The shared foundation is economic reasoning, statistics, empirical design, and clear writing. AI specialization adds enough technical fluency to measure the treatment, audit model-assisted outputs, and connect technology changes to economic mechanisms. Python, R or Stata, SQL, version control, and a reproducible writing sample are useful ways to show those skills.

That includes inference and training compute, accelerators and networking, cloud capacity, data center space, and power. This creates demand for economic work on pricing, investment, capacity, and procurement. Employers advertise some of this work under economist titles and some under finance, strategy, or planning titles. These examples demonstrate hiring routes. They do not measure the size or growth of the job market.

### Qualifications, progression, and compensation

- **Analyst or associate.** Demonstrate SQL, spreadsheet or Python modeling, unit economics, and scenario analysis. Build a forecast tied to an investment or capacity decision. Research-track economist postings may set different degree requirements from adjacent analyst roles.
- **Economist, senior analyst, or finance lead.** Own a demand forecast, investment case, cost model, or pricing study. Show how you handled utilization uncertainty, engineering constraints, and forecast errors.
- **Principal, director, or head of function.** Own portfolio trade-offs across products, regions, and capacity commitments. Lead decisions on pricing, capital allocation, or procurement with engineering and commercial teams. The leadership title depends on the mandate.
- **Disclosed pay example.** OpenAI's San Francisco Strategic Finance, Compute posting disclosed **$234,000–$260,000 base plus equity**. This is a single adjacent finance-role posting. Use it alongside the broader compensation table, with location, level, and pay components kept explicit. [Employer posting](https://openai.com/careers/strategic-finance-compute-san-francisco/)

### Connect the layers in one analysis

Start with completed tasks and translate them into billable tokens by category. Translate inference load into accelerator-hours using benchmarked throughput, batching, and utilization. Convert peak capacity into power needs using equipment draw, cooling and other facility overhead, and spare capacity. Forecast training loads separately. Token prices are customer charges. The provider's cost depends on the resources used, fixed commitments, and operating costs.

A fall in compute required per task can lower the cost of serving a given workload. Total compute demand can still rise if lower prices or better models expand usage enough. A useful sensitivity test varies task volume, tokens per task, throughput, and peak utilization independently, then checks when extra capacity becomes economical. For a financial economist, that work connects demand forecasts and unit costs to capital budgeting and return on invested capital.

## Portfolio evidence for a financial economist entering AI economics

- **Economist-level evidence.** Produce a reproducible study of AI-assisted financial workflow costs and accuracy using synthetic or public data. Compare manual and AI-assisted production, then validate both against a common rubric. Start from the [workflow economics pilot](../projects/ai-workflow-economics/README.md).
- **Senior-level evidence.** Turn that design into an independent research stream with a decision brief, a technical appendix, a replication package, a documented external review, and a follow-up test in another workflow.
- **Principal or chief-level evidence.** Publish a coherent research agenda that connects firm investment returns, worker impacts, model risk, and governance. Document where the work changed a decision, and show experience setting priorities across teams.
