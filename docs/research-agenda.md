# Research agenda

| Field | Economic question | Starting method and output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labor and education | Which tasks change, who captures productivity gains, and how do wages and skills adjust? | Task exposure maps, randomized workflows, occupation panels, and a labor brief |
| Microeconomics and firms | When does AI adoption improve unit economics, quality, or consumer surplus? | Cost models, workflow trials, demand estimation, and an adoption decision note |
| Industrial organization | How do compute costs, distribution, switching costs, and data access shape competition? | Market definition, procurement comparisons, and a competition memo |
| Finance and insurance | When does spending produce adequate returns, and how does AI affect underwriting, model risk, or asset management? | Capital budgeting, scenario analysis, and a model validation or investment memo |
| Macroeconomics | Under what adoption and diffusion paths could AI change productivity, investment, and inflation? | Growth accounting, scenario ranges, and a forecast note |
| Public economics and regulation | Who bears the costs of AI errors, and which policy instruments could improve outcomes? | Welfare analysis, incidence, implementation analysis, and a policy brief |
| Development and international economics | How do language access, infrastructure, trade, and institutions affect who benefits? | Cross-country comparisons, local workflow pilots, and a development brief |
| Infrastructure and environment | How do electricity, grid capacity, and compute costs affect deployment? | Cost curves, sensitivity analysis, and an infrastructure note |
| Behavioral and welfare economics | When does reliance on AI improve decisions or increase overconfidence? | Randomized decision tasks, calibration measures, and a decision-design brief |

For pricing, compute, data centers, and power research questions, see [derived demand across the AI supply chain](ai-economist-career-ladder.md#derived-demand-for-economists-across-the-ai-supply-chain).

Prioritize projects by decision relevance, data access, and whether a credible comparison is feasible. The first project starts with a measurable workflow before moving to claims about firms or the wider economy.
