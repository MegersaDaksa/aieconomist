# AI economics reading list, oldest to newest

A curated bibliography for studying the economics of AI and using AI in economic research. Sources reviewed through November 18, 2026. Dates refer to the linked publication or issue, or the first issue of a working paper where indicated. Journal articles, books, institutional reports, and working papers are labeled separately. Early readings provide economic foundations that predate modern AI.

| Year | Reading | Type | Why read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1930 | J. M. Keynes, [Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchildren](https://olli.gmu.edu/docstore/400docs/0906-402-John%20Maynard%20Keynes%201930.pdf) | Essay | Frame the long-run question of whether productivity gains translate into leisure, higher output, or transitional unemployment. |
| 1957 | Robert Solow, [Technical Change and the Aggregate Production Function](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1926047) | Review of Economics and Statistics | Learn growth accounting and why measured productivity is a starting point for investigating technology's contribution. |
| 1962 | Kenneth Arrow, [Economic Welfare and the Allocation of Resources for Invention](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/rate-and-direction-inventive-activity-economic-and-social-factors/economic-welfare-and-allocation-resources-invention) | Book chapter | Analyze appropriability, uncertainty, and the incentives to invest in knowledge. |
| 1995 | Timothy Bresnahan and Manuel Trajtenberg, [General Purpose Technologies ‘Engines of Growth’?](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076%2894%2901598-t) | Journal of Econometrics | Ask when complementary innovations and adoption across industries turn a technology into an engine of growth. |
| 2003 | David Autor, Frank Levy, and Richard Murnane, [The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552801) | Quarterly Journal of Economics | Use tasks as the unit of analysis for understanding how technology changes jobs and skill demand. |
| 2017 | Sendhil Mullainathan and Jann Spiess, [Machine Learning: An Applied Econometric Approach](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.31.2.87) | Journal of Economic Perspectives | Decide whether a research problem calls for out-of-sample prediction, parameter estimation, or a design that identifies causal effects. |
| 2018 | Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans, and Avi Goldfarb, [Prediction Machines](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/prediction-machines/9781633695689/) | Book, Harvard Business Review Press | Use the falling cost of prediction to examine the value of judgment and the economics of redesigning decisions. |
| 2019 | Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo, [Automation and New Tasks](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25684) | Journal of Economic Perspectives, linked working-paper version | Examine how displacement and new task creation affect labor demand and labor's share of income. |
| 2019 | Susan Athey and Guido Imbens, [Machine Learning Methods That Economists Should Know About](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-economics-080217-053433) | Annual Review of Economics | See how machine learning can support empirical work on treatment effects, policy choice, and counterfactuals. |
| 2019 | Agrawal, Gans, and Goldfarb, eds., [The Economics of Artificial Intelligence: An Agenda](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/economics-artificial-intelligence-agenda) | Edited volume, University of Chicago Press | Start with the introduction, then select chapters on growth, jobs, inequality, regulation, and economic research. |
| 2020 | Acemoglu and Restrepo, [Robots and Jobs: Evidence from US Labor Markets](https://doi.org/10.1086/705716) | Journal of Political Economy | Study an empirical design linking automation exposure to wages and employment in local labor markets. |
| 2020 | Emilio Calvano, Giacomo Calzolari, Vincenzo Denicolò, and Sergio Pastorello, [Artificial Intelligence, Algorithmic Pricing, and Collusion](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623) | American Economic Review | Examine how simulated learning agents can reach supracompetitive prices and which assumptions matter for applying the result to real markets. |
| 2021 | Erik Brynjolfsson, Daniel Rock, and Chad Syverson, [The Productivity J-Curve](https://doi.org/10.1257/mac.20180386) | American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics | Study how spending on complementary intangible capital can affect measured productivity over time. |
| 2022 | Andreas Fuster, Paul Goldsmith-Pinkham, Tarun Ramadorai, and Ansgar Walther, [Predictably Unequal? The Effects of Machine Learning on Credit Markets](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13090) | Journal of Finance | Ask how changes in credit prediction affect the distribution of loan pricing and access across borrowers. |
| 2023 | Acemoglu and Simon Johnson, [Power and Progress](https://search.worldcat.org/title/1345216228) | Book, PublicAffairs | Examine how institutional choices and bargaining power shape who benefits from technological change. |
| 2023 | Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang, [Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586) | Science | Use the randomized professional-writing experiment to design separate measures of time and output quality. |
| 2023 | Anton Korinek, [Generative AI for Economic Research](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20231736) | Journal of Economic Literature | Build practical workflows for coding, writing, synthesis, and research assistance. Define how each output will be checked. |
| 2024 | Mauro Cazzaniga et al., [Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/staff-discussion-notes/Issues/2024/01/14/Gen-AI-Artificial-Intelligence-and-the-Future-of-Work-542379) | IMF Staff Discussion Note | Compare occupational exposure, complementarity, and preparedness across economies. |
| 2024 | Alex Kim, Maximilian Muhn, and Valeri Nikolaev, [Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17866) | Preprint, linked version | Design a point-in-time test of earnings-direction forecasts using financial statements. Compare forecast accuracy and economic value separately. |
| 2024 | IMF, [Advances in Artificial Intelligence: Implications for Capital Market Activities](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2024/october/english/ch3.pdf) | Global Financial Stability Report chapter | Identify channels through which AI adoption could affect trading, liquidity, and financial stability. |
| 2025 | Acemoglu, [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/0fcf7575-a092-4728-a0c5-077beda38427) | Economic Policy | Trace how task exposure, cost savings, and the economic weight of affected tasks enter aggregate productivity estimates. |
| 2025 | Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey Raymond, [Generative AI at Work](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044) | Quarterly Journal of Economics | Examine effects on customer-support productivity and how gains vary across workers in a real deployment. |
| 2025 | Anders Humlum and Emilie Vestergaard, [Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777) | NBER working paper | Compare AI adoption and changes in tasks with measured wages and hours in Danish administrative data. |
| 2025 | International Labour Organization, [Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure) | ILO working paper | Use an occupational exposure measure as a starting point for cross-country comparisons. Track adoption and employment outcomes separately. |
| 2026 | [Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a Randomized Experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851) | NBER working paper, February issue | Consider how to measure differences by education and include an unassisted follow-up to investigate learning. |
| 2026 | [Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34984) | NBER working paper, March issue | Compare executive-reported adoption, productivity, and expectations with measures from worker-level experiments. |
| 2026 | Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin, David Deming, and Tyler Schumacher, [What Work Does Generative AI Do?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677) | NBER working paper, August issue | Examine reported task-level use and how adoption measures can inform labor-market analysis. |

## Read first for the repository's projects

1. **Workflow pilot.** Read Noy and Zhang, then Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond. Use their designs to refine time and quality measures in the [AI workflow economics pilot](../projects/ai-workflow-economics/README.md). Track drafting, review, and rework separately.
2. **Labor and macro.** Read Autor, Levy, and Murnane, then the ILO index, Humlum and Vestergaard, and Acemoglu's macro paper. Record which measures capture exposure, adoption, job outcomes, or modeled aggregate effects.
3. **Finance and insurance.** Read Fuster et al., then the IMF capital-markets chapter. Use Kim et al. to frame a point-in-time validation exercise for a financial analysis task.
4. **Research practice.** Pair Mullainathan and Spiess with Korinek. Choose an economic research task, define a benchmark, and measure prediction quality or research accuracy on held-out cases.

For each reading note, record the economic question, unit of analysis, data vintage, method, principal claim, scope for generalization, and one test or decision it informs. Check newer versions of working papers before using their estimates in public work.
